# Blasphemy

The Greek blasphemy word family can describe speech that reviles or defames God or people; context identifies the target. Scripture also describes conduct that dishonors or profanes God's name and can lead others to blaspheme. This article distinguishes those acts while exploring how conduct affects the honor of God's name.

## Defining Blasphemy

According to the [Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/blasphemy):

> something that you say or do that shows you do not respect God or a religion — Cambridge Dictionary

The Greek word family has a wider range of targets than this English definition suggests. Titus 3:2 uses the verb for speaking evil of people, and Romans 3:8 describes slander against Paul and his companions. Jude 1:9 mentions a blasphemous judgment during a dispute with the devil, but does not explicitly name its target. The word alone does not identify someone as divine.

That broad English definition does not establish that every disrespectful deed is called *blasphemy* in a biblical passage. In Matthew 12:24–32, the Pharisees **say** that Jesus casts out demons by Beelzebul; Jesus replies that he casts them out by the Spirit of God and warns against blasphemy against the Spirit. The immediate example is a spoken accusation about God's work, not merely a failure to live faithfully.

## Hypocrisy

The apostle Paul says that those who claim to teach God's law dishonor God when they break it. Because of their hypocrisy, God's name is blasphemed among the Gentiles. Paul distinguishes their conduct from the blasphemy it provokes.

> But if you call yourself a Jew and rely on the law and boast in God and know his will and approve what is excellent, because you are instructed from the law; and if you are sure that you yourself are a guide to the blind, a light to those who are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of children, having in the law the embodiment of knowledge and truth— you then who teach others, do you not teach yourself? While you preach against stealing, do you steal? You who say that one must not commit adultery, do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols, do you rob temples?
>
> You who boast in the law dishonor God by breaking the law. For, as it is written,
>
>> “The [name](name.md) of God is **blasphemed** among the Gentiles because of you.”
>
> — Romans 2:17-24 (ESV)

Such conduct is still serious. When David sinned, Nathan said that his deed scorned the LORD; this passage describes David's act as scorn, not as an instance of the Greek noun *blasphēmia*.

> David said to Nathan, “I have sinned against the LORD.”  
> And Nathan said to David, “The LORD also has put away your sin; you shall not die. Nevertheless, because **by this deed you have utterly scorned** the LORD, the child who is born to you shall die.”
>
> — 2 Samuel 12:13-14 (ESV)

## Profaning the Holy Name

Biblical history shows that God's name is more important than the comfort of a nation. When Israel defiled their land and was scattered among the nations, their conduct continued to reflect poorly on God.

> The word of the LORD came to me: “Son of man, when the house of Israel lived in their own land, they defiled it by their ways and their deeds. Their ways before me were like the uncleanness of a woman in her menstrual impurity. So I poured out My wrath upon them for the blood that they had shed in the land, for the idols with which they had defiled it. I scattered them among the nations, and they were dispersed through the countries. In accordance with their ways and their deeds I judged them. But when they came to the nations, wherever they came, **they profaned My [holy](holy.md) [name](name.md)**. This occurred when people said of them, ‘These are the people of the LORD, and yet they had to go out of his land.’ But I had concern for **My holy name, which the house of Israel had profaned** among the nations to which they came.
>
> “Therefore say to the house of Israel, Thus says the Lord GOD: It is not for your sake, O house of Israel, that I am about to act, but for the sake of my holy name, which you have profaned among the nations to which you came. And I will sanctify *(set apart as holy)* **My great name, which has been profaned** among the nations, and which you have profaned among them. And the nations will know that I am [the LORD](https://ofgod.info/name), declares the Lord GOD, when through you I vindicate My holiness before their eyes.
>
> — Ezekiel 36:16-23 (ESV)

## The Third Commandment

The importance of respecting God's name is rooted in the Ten Commandments.

> You shall not take **the [name](name.md) of [the LORD](https://ofgod.info/name)** your God in vain, for the LORD will not hold him guiltless who takes **His name** in vain. — Exodus 20:7 (ESV)

## Defense Against Misconceptions

The [dictionary definition](#defining-blasphemy) includes disrespectful actions, but the passages here use more precise descriptions. Matthew 12:24–32 addresses a spoken accusation against God's Spirit. [Romans 2](#hypocrisy) says that lawbreaking dishonors God and causes his name to be blasphemed; [Ezekiel 36](#profaning-the-holy-name) calls Israel's treatment of God's name *profanation*. An action can dishonor God and provoke blasphemous speech without the passage calling that action blasphemy. This distinction does not make hypocrisy less serious.

## Conclusion

The [Greek word can describe slander against people as well as God](#defining-blasphemy); its target depends on context. Hypocrisy is related but distinct: it [dishonors God and can cause others to blaspheme](#hypocrisy). Scripture also describes conduct that [profanes God's name](#profaning-the-holy-name). Both speech and conduct matter, but the passages should not be made to say that every dishonorable deed is itself blasphemy.
