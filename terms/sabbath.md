# Sabbath

This article examines biblical teaching about the Sabbath. It considers the day of observance, the meaning of work, and activities associated with worship, mercy, and rest.

## Day of Observance

The Sabbath is [not Sunday](https://church.ofgod.info/terms/sundays). It falls on the seventh day of the week, corresponding to Saturday in the modern Gregorian calendar.

## Command

The Sabbath is presented as a command rather than an optional practice. [The Sabbath was commanded.](https://church.ofgod.info/terms/sundays)

## Defining Work

The Hebrew word for “work” is [*melakah* (מְלָאכָה)](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/4399.htm) and is prohibited in the fourth commandment (Exodus 20:10).

Melāʾkhāh (מְלָאכָה) means work or occupation. Depending on context, it may refer to business, a task or work performed, workmanship, service, or public duties.

## Shopping

This interpretation holds that shopping conflicts with the Sabbath command for two reasons:

- **It causes others to work.** Exodus 20:10 states that male and female servants, livestock, and foreigners within the gates must rest. Commercial buying requires work by shopkeepers, transport workers, and merchants.
- **It is commercial trade.** Nehemiah 10:31 and 13:15–22 address the buying of food, merchandise, and goods on the Sabbath. Nehemiah closed the city gates to stop buying and selling, describing Sabbath commerce as a profanation of the day.

## Personal Pleasure

Older English renderings of Isaiah 58:13–14 translate the Hebrew word *chephets* as “pleasure”. For example:

> If you turn back your foot from the Sabbath, from doing **your [pleasure](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/2656.htm)** on My holy day, and call the Sabbath a [delight](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/6027.htm) and the holy day of the LORD honorable;
> if you honor it, not going your own ways, or seeking your own pleasure, or talking idly;
> then you shall take delight in the LORD, and I will make you ride on the heights of the earth; I will feed you with the heritage of Jacob your father, for the mouth of the LORD has spoken.”
>
> Isaiah 58:13–14 (ESV)

This wording has led some readers to understand the passage as prohibiting wholesome joy, recreation, or personal happiness on the Sabbath.

The Hebrew word translated as “pleasure” is *chephets* (חֵפֶץ). The [Brown-Driver-Briggs Hebrew Lexicon](https://biblehub.com/bdb/2656.htm), [Gesenius’ Hebrew Grammar and Lexicon](https://biblehub.com/gesenius/2656.htm), and [Bible Hub’s Hebrew entry](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/2656.htm) include meanings such as “matter”, “business”, “affair”, and “commercial enterprise”.

This interpretation understands *chephets* in Isaiah 58:3 and 58:13 as personal business or affairs rather than entertainment. BDB Definition 3 lists “matter, business, affair” and cites Isaiah 58:3,13 and Ecclesiastes 3:1. The NET Bible renders the phrase as “pursuing your own affairs”, while the Complete Jewish Bible translates it as “pursuing your own interests”.

Isaiah 58 critiques religious hypocrisy together with social and economic injustice. In Isaiah 58:3, God addresses fasting while people seek their business and oppress their labourers. This reading applies the same concern to Isaiah 58:13, understanding the prohibition as conducting normal commercial affairs on the Sabbath.

The passage also calls the Sabbath a **delight**:

> *“and call the Sabbath a **delight** [[עֹנֶג. *‘ōneḡ*](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/6027.htm)] and the holy day of the Lord honorable...”* — Isaiah 58:13 (ESV)

The [Brown-Driver-Briggs Lexicon](https://biblehub.com/bdb/6027.htm) defines *‘ōneḡ* (עֹנֶג) in terms of delight, luxury, and refreshment. This reading therefore contrasts self-serving commercial ambition with divine rest and shared celebratory peace.

## Expectation of Sabbath

### Communal Worship and Sacred Gathering

The primary positive command for the Sabbath in the Torah is to hold a holy convocation, or *miqra kodesh*:

> “Six days shall [work](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/4399.htm) be done, but on the seventh day is a [Sabbath](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7676.htm) of [solemn rest](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/7677.htm), a holy [convocation](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/4744.htm). You shall do no [work](https://biblehub.com/hebrew/4399.htm). It is a Sabbath to the LORD in all your dwelling places. — Leviticus 23:3 (ESV)

The word *miqra* means a public assembly or reading. The day was intended for communities to gather for worship and sacred focus, rather than for isolation or inactivity.

### Scripture Reading and Teaching

Biblical accounts portray the Sabbath as a day for instruction and spiritual growth:

- **Visiting prophets and teachers.** In 2 Kings 4:23, a woman’s husband asks why she is visiting the prophet Elisha when it is neither New Moon nor Sabbath. This suggests that visiting a teacher or prophet on the Sabbath was customary.
- **Synagogue teaching.** Jesus read and explained Scripture to gathered communities on the Sabbath (Luke 4:16; Mark 1:21).
- **Reasoning and discussion.** Paul spent Sabbaths in synagogues reasoning with Jews and Greeks about the Scriptures (Acts 13:14; 17:2; 18:4).

### Mercy, Relief, and Healing

Jesus challenged the view that Sabbath rest required complete inactivity. He stated that “it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath” (Matthew 12:12). His Sabbath works included:

- **Healing the sick.** He restored a man’s withered hand, released a woman who had been crippled for eighteen years, and healed a paralysed man at Bethesda (Matthew 12:9–13; Luke 13:10–17; John 5:1–9).
- **Relieving suffering.** Jesus argued that releasing a person from illness or affliction on the Sabbath fulfilled the day’s purpose (Luke 13:16).

### Sacred Service and Temple Duties

For those serving in worship, the Sabbath involved active labour rather than physical stillness:

- **Additional sacrifices.** Numbers 28:9–10 required priests to offer additional lambs, grain, and drink offerings on the Sabbath.
- **Preparing showbread.** Levites baked and arranged fresh Bread of the Presence every Sabbath (1 Chronicles 9:32; Leviticus 24:8).
- **Priestly service.** Jesus noted that priests in the temple “profane the Sabbath and are guiltless” because sacred service takes place in God’s presence (Matthew 12:5).

### Feasting, Joy, and Hospitality

The biblical vision of Sabbath rest includes physical delight and shared meals:

- **Calling the day a delight.** Isaiah 58:13 calls Israel to “call the Sabbath a delight and the holy day of the LORD honourable”. The day is presented as a time for joy, special attire, and good food.
- **Shared hospitality.** Jesus attended a Sabbath meal at the house of a prominent Pharisee (Luke 14:1). Preparing double portions beforehand, as described in Exodus 16:22, allowed families to feast and host guests without cooking that day.

### Animal Welfare and Preserving Life

Biblical teaching and Jesus’ examples identify essential care and the preservation of life as appropriate Sabbath activities:

- **Watering and rescuing livestock.** Jesus noted that people untie an ox or donkey to lead it to water on the Sabbath and would pull a sheep from a pit (Luke 13:15; Matthew 12:11).
- **Covenant rites.** Circumcision was performed on the Sabbath when it was a child’s eighth day, so that the covenant rite was not delayed (John 7:22–23).
