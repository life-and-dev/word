# Heaven

Many people believe heaven is some mysterious place in the sky where God lives. Commonly people assume that when your body die, your ["immortal" soul](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/immortal) will continue to live there. Often artists would create pictures of golden gates in clouds as some sort of entrance into "heaven".  These ideas potentially originated from the Greek mythology that described [Elysium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elysium) as such a place where heroes and gods continue to live after death.

However, the bible state that when we die [we will be dead](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death). That is why we need to trust God that we will be [resurrected](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/resurrection) like Jesus.

## Elijah going to Heaven

Many people believed Elijah went directly to this kind of heaven without dying based on the scripture:

> And as they still went on and talked, behold, chariots of fire and horses of fire separated the two of them. And Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven. And Elisha saw it and he cried, “My father, my father! The chariots of Israel and its horsemen!” And he saw him no more. — 2 Kings 11:12 (ESV)

The original Israeli who lived in Elijah's time were not influenced by Greek mythology like us. To them, "heaven" is the sky.  For example:

> He blotted out every living thing that was on the face of the ground, man and animals and creeping things and birds of **the heavens**. — Genesis 7:23 (ESV)

and

> And at the seventh time he said, “Behold, a little cloud like a man's hand is rising from the sea.”
> And he said, “Go up, say to Ahab, ‘Prepare your chariot and go down, lest the rain stop you.’”
>
> And in a little while **the heavens** grew black with clouds and wind, and there was a great rain.
>
> — 1 Kings 18:44-45 (ESV)

and

> For as the rain and the snow come down from **heaven** and do not return there but water the earth... — Isaiah 55:10 (ESV)

The apostles also referred to "heaven" as the sky. For example:

> We also are men, of like nature with you, and we bring you good news, that you should turn from these vain things to a living God, Who made **the heaven** and the earth and the sea and all that is in them. In past generations he allowed all the nations to walk in their own ways. Yet he did not leave himself without witness, for he did good by giving you rains from **heaven** ... — Acts 14:15-17 (ESV)

The prophets did not consider that Elijah has entered some sort of realms of gods, but instead they thought he would eventually be set down somewhere else. That is why they looked for him (2 Kings 2:16).

> While others wanted to search for Elijah, Elisha understood that if God had wanted Elijah there, He would not have moved him. Understanding the customs of the time further helps to clarify this section of Scripture. God had told Elijah to anoint Elisha to succeed him (1 Kings 19:16). According to custom, Elisha could not assume leadership of the prophets as long as Elijah was present. Therefore, in order to allow Elisha to lead, God had to find a way to graciously remove Elijah. He chose to do it by a whirlwind. Elijah was not taken up to the place where God lives, but he was taken up into the air and placed somewhere else. God simply moved Elijah from one place to another and he lived out his days somewhere else. Elijah could not have been taken to heaven to live with God because he lived before the time of Christ. It is Christ’s shed blood that is the only valid payment for all men’s sin, including Elijah’s. If Elijah were already enjoying everlasting life in heaven, there was no need for Christ to come and to die for his sin. — [Spirit and Truth International](https://spiritandtruthonline.org/verses-sometimes-used-to-support-the-idea-that-our-everlasting-future-is-in-heaven/)

## Rewards in heaven

### Matthew

Some verses mention a reward in heaven like:

> Rejoice and be glad, for **your reward is great in heaven**... — Matthew 5:12 (ESV)

and

> Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal, but lay up for yourselves **treasures in heaven**, where neither moth nor rust destroys and where thieves do not break in and steal. — Matthew 6:19-20 (ESV)

Out of context, it would seem that we would go immediately to heaven to receive our rewards when we die.

Matthew call God's Kingdom the "Kingdom of Heaven". So when he refers to "in heaven", he meant "the Kingdom of Heaven". In that context, you will notice that Matthew 5 starts with:

> Seeing the crowds, he went up on the mountain, and when he sat down, his disciples came to him. And he opened his mouth and taught them, saying:
>
> "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is **the kingdom of heaven**."
>
> ...
>
> "Blessed are those who are persecuted for righteousness' sake, for theirs is **the kingdom of heaven**."
>
> — Matthew 5:1-3,10 (ESV)

Then Jesus continues to teach about the kingdom of heaven (Matthew 5:19,20) all the way until Matthew 7:21. Often Matthew shortens "the kingdom of heaven" to "heaven" because he did not expect us to quote his book out of context.

### Apostles

However, there are more verses written by the apostles like:

> ... the hope laid up for you in **heaven**. — Colossians 1:5 (ESV)

and

> According to His great mercy, He has caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead, to an inheritance that is imperishable, undefiled, and unfading, **kept in heaven for you** ... — 1 Peter 1:3-4 (ESV)

However, before the apostles' times, Malachi prophesied:

> Then those who feared the LORD spoke with one another. The LORD paid attention and heard them, and **a book of remembrance** was written before Him of those who feared the LORD and esteemed His name.
>
> "They shall be Mine", says the LORD of hosts, **in the day** when I make up My treasured possession, and I will spare them as a man spares his son who serves him. **Then** once more you shall see the distinction between the righteous and the wicked, between one who serves God and one who does not serve Him.
>
> — Malachi 3:16-18 (ESV)

The Israeli knew there would first come [a day of judgement and **then** the reward would follow](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death).

Solomon also warned about a judgment:

> For God will bring every deed into judgment, with every secret thing, whether good or evil. — Ecclesiastes 12:14 (ESV)

Even the book of Revelation warns of this judgement (Revelation 20:12).

There are no scripture that indicate we will receive instant heavenly gratification when we die. This kind of reasoning comes from movies, cartoons and books which was influenced by unbiblical folk tales and Greek methodology.

Possible explanations:

* [&#34;Heaven&#34; is a Hebrew idiom](https://eternal.family.net.za/bible/idioms) for something that is good. So when the reward is "in heaven", it means it is a very good reward.
* Our rewards are not material gifts, but instead it is God's respect and love for us. Jesus referred to God as "Our Father in Heaven" and our reward is **to be with Him**.
* "Heaven" is some sort of protective dimension or place where God safely store our rewards in our absence for the time when we are able to receive it.

Whatever you believe your "reward" is, it is clear that it is safe with God "Who is in heaven". In other words, nobody on Earth can steal, damage or even touch our rewards because it is not affected by the events on Earth.

## God's kingdom on Earth

After all Jesus teachings and after his resurrection. The disciples did not expect Jesus to ascent into heaven. They expected him to establish his Kingdom on Earth, instead of Heaven, when they asked him:

> So when they had come together, they asked him, “Lord, will you at this time restore the kingdom to Israel?” — Acts 1:6 (ESV)

## The Father's house

The NIV bible incorrectly translate the manuscripts as:

> My Father’s house has many *[rooms](https://biblehub.com/greek/3438.htm)*; if that were not so, would I have told you that I am going there to prepare a place for you? And if I go and prepare a *[place](https://biblehub.com/greek/5117.htm)* for you, I will come back and ***take you to be with me*** that you also may be where I am. — John 14:2-3 (NIV)

This gives the impression that Jesus would come back and take us to heaven when we die. If we had [immortal souls](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/immortal) and Jesus has to come back and commute every soul instantly to heaven every time a Christian dies, then Jesus would have returned many times already and not according to the prophecies of his second coming.

The King James Version state:

> In my Father's house are many *[mansions](https://biblehub.com/greek/3438.htm)*: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a *[place](https://biblehub.com/greek/5117.htm)* for you, I will come again, and **receive you unto myself**; that where I am, there ye may be also. — John 14:2-3 (KJV)

[“Dwelling places”](https://biblehub.com/greek/3438.htm) is translated as "mansions" in the KJV. The meaning of the Greek word *μονή* does not mean modern luxury “mansions”. Jesus is not a slow home builder to whom we have to wait thousands of years to finish his project.

**We** are the Father's dwelling place and Jesus is **preparing us** for God's glory and purpose.

> “In him **you also are being built** together into **a dwelling place for God** by the Spirit.” — Ephesians 2:22 (ESV)

Another example is where Paul referred to the church as the "bride" of Christ:

> Husbands, love your wives, as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her, that **he might sanctify her**, having **cleansed her by the washing of water with the word**, so that **he might present the church to himself in splendor**, without spot or wrinkle or any such thing, that **she might be holy and without blemish**. — Ephesians 5:25-27 (ESV)

## Heavenly Citizenship

Philippians 3:20 is often discussed when considering whether believers go to heaven at death. Paul places heavenly citizenship within a contrast between two ways of life and their outcomes:

> For many, of whom I have often told you and now tell you even with tears, walk as enemies of the cross of Christ. Their end is [destruction](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death), their god is their belly, and they glory in their shame, with **minds set on earthly things**. But **our citizenship is in heaven**, and from it we await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, who will transform our lowly body to be like his glorious body, by the power that enables him even to subject all things to himself. — Philippians 3:18-21 (ESV)

The Greek word translated “citizenship,” [πολίτευμα](https://biblehub.com/greek/4175.htm), refers to citizenship or a commonwealth. Paul contrasts believers’ heavenly **identity** with the earthly orientation of those whose conduct opposes Christ’s [crucifixion](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/christ/crucifixion).

Heavenly citizenship therefore concerns **belonging and allegiance**, which should shape believers’ conduct. It does not mean they are already physically in heaven, nor going to heaven when they die.

Earlier, Paul expresses his desire to attain the **resurrection from the dead** (Philippians 3:10-11). He then describes Jesus transforming believers’ lowly bodies to be like his glorious body (Philippians 3:21). Elsewhere, Paul explicitly describes resurrection life as imperishable and immortal (1 Corinthians 15:42-54). These promises concern renewed bodily life; they do not say that glorious bodies are needed specifically to live in heaven.

[Deceased believers are unconscious until resurrection](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death#death-is-like-sleeping). Within that understanding, they await life again rather than consciously experiencing heavenly citizenship after death. This fits Paul’s description of the dead rising when the Lord descends from heaven (1 Thessalonians 4:13-18).

## The Coming World

The Greek noun [αἰών (*aiōn*)](https://biblehub.com/greek/165.htm) commonly means an age or era. Older translations sometimes render it “world”, which can obscure the time-related meaning for modern readers. For example, the KJV’s “end of the world” becomes “end of the age” in the ESV (Matthew 24:3), while “the world to come” becomes “the age to come” (Mark 10:30).

These expressions distinguish the present age from a coming age. They do not, by themselves, describe believers moving to another planet or entering heaven. However, “world” is not incorrect in every context: αἰών can also describe the order associated with an age, so each passage requires contextual examination.

## Conclusion

“Heaven” does not have one meaning in every passage. It can refer to the [visible sky](#elijah-going-to-heaven), while other passages speak of [God’s heavenly dwelling and the rewards kept safe with him](#rewards-in-heaven). Context determines the meaning. Neither rewards stored in heaven nor [heavenly citizenship](#heavenly-citizenship) automatically establishes that believers move there when they die. Heavenly citizenship expresses their belonging and allegiance to God, rather than their present physical location.

Within the understanding of death presented here, [deceased believers remain unconscious until resurrection](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death#death-is-like-sleeping). Their hope is not continued conscious life as disembodied souls, but being raised when Jesus returns. The promise is that Jesus will [transform their lowly bodies to be like his glorious body](#heavenly-citizenship) which will be [imperishable](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/resurrection#the-resurrection-body).

Christ’s work also includes [sanctifying his people and preparing them as God’s dwelling](#the-fathers-house). The central hope is lasting life with Jesus and the Father. The resurrection article places that hope in [God’s renewed creation](https://kingdom.ofgod.info/death/resurrection#explaining-the-resurrection), rather than treating heaven as an automatic destination immediately after death.
